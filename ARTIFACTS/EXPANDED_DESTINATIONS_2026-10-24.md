## Editorial rule

A destination earns a URL only if it answers a different question, contains a substantive original explanation and a useful decision object, and sends a reader to one or two next questions in context. Google Search Central says internal links should have concise descriptive anchors, not a quota; NN/g says the label and surrounding cue should predict the destination. Avoid multiple thin pages built by swapping headings. The visual system changes with the job: a calendar, annotated receipt, conversation transcript, street map, stock ledger, order slip, buyer comparison, or source image. No star motifs, numbered decorative chapter markers or oversized empty hero panels. Never copy IdeaProof prose or layout.

Each proposed page begins with a search-intent H1 matching its descriptive slug, then answers the first question above the fold. The column beside prose holds the decision object, not blank space. Source posts/guides, real taxonomy or published ideas supply examples; additional claims require independent verification before publication. Do not expose a planned route in navigation until its full content and destination QA exist. All preview HTML and images remain noindex.
